About

Engineering meets business strategy.

Zolomon Kaliser is the founder of ZKK Consulting LLC. He holds a B.S. in Finance from UT Dallas and is completing a Master of Engineering in Biomedical Engineering at Texas A&M University (expected May 2027), an accelerated path that began when he started college at 14.

At ZKK he leads market analysis and financial modeling across industries: competitive landscapes, market sizing, pricing and go-to-market research, and multi-year revenue and cost models. He also carries the firm's medical device and FDA-related research, where the combination of clinical engineering and commercial training lets a single engagement move between the technical and the market picture.

His work on ZKK's internal Vyrex wearable program extended that range into hardware economics: bill-of-materials cost engineering, vendor and assembler sourcing, and the unit-cost modeling that determines whether a device thesis survives contact with a real supply chain.

About

Engineering meets market research.

Jad Haddad leads ZKK Consulting's technology and product research: electronics, embedded systems, connected devices, component and supply-chain landscapes, and the standards and certification requirements that shape what a hardware product can actually claim. He works the side of an engagement where the question is not just how large a market is, but whether the product can be built, sourced, certified, and sold at the price the model assumes.

His research approach comes out of hardware development rather than finance. Before ZKK, he originated the concept for and led the technical design and R&D on Vyrex, an internal wrist-worn fall-detection wearable program that produced a PCB design carried to Version 3.0.0, a bench prototype, a full sourcing migration across two supply chains, and a prior-art review. That program is the reason ZKK's hardware engagements include a bill of materials that reflects real vendor stock and real minimum order quantities rather than a spreadsheet of list prices.

Engineering Projects

Vyrex Wrist Wearable

Originated the concept for and led the technical design and R&D on an internal wrist-worn fall-detection wearable program. Directed the hardware architecture: a PCB design carried through Version 3.0.0, a bench prototype built on an ESP32 development board with an Adafruit LSM6DSOXTR six-axis IMU breakout, and a complete component specification covering BLE MCU, inertial sensing, lithium-polymer charge management, USB-C power input, regulation, and MOSFET-driven haptic feedback with flyback protection. Led the sensor migration from the STM LSM6DSOXTR to the LSM6DSV16XTR and the ESP32 devkit to an nRF52805 BLE 5.4 module, weighing assembler stock availability, power consumption, pin-compatibility, and researched prior art and competitive patents in the fall-detection wearable space to inform the the program's IP strategy. Vyrex was an internal research program and was not commercialized.
